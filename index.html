THE FAIR QUESTION

Why not just Claude and a good prompt?

You can. Nothing here is magic, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling something. What you’d be doing is building the thing below, on your own time, and then finding out on live deals which parts of it were wrong.

69 files, about 37,000 words
That’s the whole kit, and you can count it after you buy. Eight agent definitions, twelve plays, thirteen skills, the brain template, the governance rules and the guides.
The routing, which is the hard part
A prompt gets you one good answer. The work is deciding which move a situation calls for and pulling the right knowledge into it, every time, without you naming a tool. That decision table took the longest to get right and it’s the difference between a system and a folder of prompts.
The refusals
Any model will confidently repeat your own unproven number back to you, because you said it. Getting one to stop and tell you it can’t prove your claim takes rules that survive a long session, and it’s the part a single prompt never holds.
What it costs you to build
A quarter of evenings, if you already know what good looks like. The certification that teaches you to build it is about nine times this price and you still do the building. This is the same work, finished, for the cost of a decent lunch for two.

BEFORE YOU BUY

The questions worth asking first.

The ones a careful buyer asks, answered where you can read them before paying rather than after.

Who is this for, exactly?
The person who produces what the revenue team says out loud. That is usually Sales Enablement or Revenue Enablement, often Product Marketing (play 07 is a product launch, and the marketer is one of the eight), and Customer Success leaders, who get their own edition with the post-sale plays and the renewal read. It is written for one person doing that job well, in a company of roughly 50 to 500 people. It is not a tool you roll out to reps, it is the thing you use to arm them.
What am I actually receiving?
A zip file. Inside it is a folder of markdown files: a company brain, twelve plays, eight agent definitions, thirteen skills, and the guides. You open that folder in the Claude desktop app and it becomes a working system. There is no app to log into and no account on our side. The files sit on your machine and stay there.
Can I use it in Claude on the web, or in Cowork, or in Gemini?
The web version of Claude won’t work, because it can’t read a folder on your computer. StoryOS is built and tested for the Claude desktop app, using its Code tab, on a paid plan. Other tools that can read a local folder may well run it, and we haven’t tested them, so we won’t claim they do. Gemini is not supported.
Why not just do this in a chat window for free?
You can get one good battlecard from a chat window in ninety seconds. What you can’t get is the second list. A chat window will happily repeat your "40% faster onboarding" back to you as fact, because you said it. StoryOS treats your claims the way it treats theirs, and tells you which of yours carry no date and no source. That’s the difference, and it’s the whole product.
Can I add my own agents and capabilities?
Yes, and that is a real feature rather than a promise. Alfred, the guide inside the kit, builds new plays, new skills and whole new specialists on request. You describe the situation you keep hitting, he proposes the play, shows you the exact change, and writes nothing until you say yes. He never touches your brain or your finished work while extending the system.
